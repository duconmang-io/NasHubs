Manage your Synology NAS from your phone

A step-by-step guide for Android and iPhone: connect to DSM, watch health, work with files and users, and reach the NAS from anywhere without opening router ports.

Updated November 19, 2026 · by duconmang

Synology makes several mobile apps, and each one covers a single job: one for files, one for photos, one for finding the NAS on your network. To see how the NAS itself is doing, or to change a setting, you usually end up in the DSM web interface on a small screen.

This guide shows how to do everyday Synology admin from one app on Android or iPhone with NasHubs, a free, independent NAS manager. It covers connecting at home, checking health, working with files, managing users and reaching the NAS when you are away.

What you need

  • A Synology NAS running DSM 7. NasHubs is tested on a DS918+; most models that run DSM 7 work.
  • A DSM account. For admin tasks such as users, packages and containers, the account must be in the administrators group.
  • An Android phone (Android 9 or later) or an iPhone or iPad.
  • A way to reach the NAS: its local IP address at home, or your own domain or QuickConnect link from anywhere.

Step 1: Install NasHubs

On Android, download the APK from the GitHub releases page, open it and allow installs from that source. Each release lists its SHA-256 if you want to check the file. On iPhone and iPad, install it from the App Store.

Step 2: Add your NAS

  1. Find how you reach the NAS. At home this is its local IP address, shown in DSM under Control Panel → Network → Network Interface or in your router's device list, for example 192.168.1.20. Away from home it can be your own domain or your QuickConnect link.
  2. Add the NAS in NasHubs. The address field accepts any of these forms:
    You haveType it like this
    Local IP address192.168.1.20:5001
    Your own domainnas.example.com or nas.example.com:8443
    A custom DSM portnas.example.com:15001
    QuickConnecthttps://yourid.quickconnect.to

    You are not limited to DSM's default ports 5000 (HTTP) and 5001 (HTTPS): add :port after the IP or domain for whatever port your DSM or reverse proxy uses. Use HTTPS whenever you can.

  3. Sign in with your DSM account. Your password is stored in the Android Keystore or the iOS Keychain on the phone and is used only to talk to your NAS.
  4. Save a second address if you have one. NasHubs remembers a local address and a remote address (domain or QuickConnect) for each NAS, so the same entry works at home and away.

Step 3: Check your NAS at a glance

The dashboard shows live CPU and RAM use, storage, temperature, running services and uptime. It answers the questions you usually open DSM for: is the NAS busy, is a volume filling up, is it running hot.

For deeper checks, the admin screens cover disks and storage pools, the resource monitor, logs, and USB devices.

Step 4: Work with files

The Files screen lists your shared folders. You can browse, upload from the phone, download, rename and create share links. NAS files also appear in your phone's own Files app, so other apps can open them directly.

Renaming a shared folder: do this only in DSM → Control Panel → Shared Folder. Renaming the top-level folder from a file manager renames it on disk but not in DSM's share settings, and the share seems to vanish. NasHubs 2.1.2 and later block rename, move and delete on shared folders for this reason; folders inside them work as usual.

Step 5: Manage users, packages and containers

From the admin screens you can review users and groups, shared folders and installed packages. If your model supports Container Manager, you can also start and stop Docker containers and install common self-hosted apps. The Docker guide covers that in detail.

Step 6: Reach your NAS when you are away

The simplest option is the address you saved in step 2: your QuickConnect link, or your own domain with its port. If you want more control, use the agent.

Opening router ports to the DSM login page exposes it to the whole internet. NasHubs takes a different route: NasHubs Agent is a small service that runs on the NAS. It signs every command with Ed25519 over TLS with a pinned certificate, and you reach it through Cloudflare Tunnel or Tailscale. Neither needs an open port on your router.

Questions

Is NasHubs an official Synology app?

No. NasHubs is an independent app that talks to DSM through its own web API. It is not affiliated with or endorsed by Synology Inc.

Is it free?

Yes. Features marked Pro unlock for free: sign in with Google in the app and send a Pro request. There is no subscription or payment.

Does my password leave my phone?

Only to sign in to your own NAS. It is stored in the Android Keystore or iOS Keychain, and nothing passes through a NasHubs server.

Can I connect with QuickConnect or a custom port?

Yes. Paste your QuickConnect link, or type an IP address or domain followed by :port, for example nas.example.com:15001. You are not limited to ports 5000 and 5001.

Which DSM versions are supported?

DSM 7. Container features need a model that supports Container Manager.
